The workflow

Paper claims cost Cascadia about 40,000 processor hours a year

Today

  • 300,000 paper, fax and PDF claims a year (assumed 5% of volume; the national average is 2%)
  • About 8 minutes each (assumed): key around 25 fields, then look up the member, the coverage and the provider
  • Days in a keying queue before adjudication
  • Errors bounce back as generic rejections, so providers redo work

With ClaimPilot

  • Every claim read, checked and matched in about 20 seconds
  • Clear cases auto-accepted as FHIR claims, with an audit trail
  • Processors see only exceptions, already explained (about 3 minutes)
  • Providers get the exact fix: “line 1 NPI fails its check digit”

Volumes, minutes and rates are assumptions for a fictitious payer. Nationally, 2% of medical claims are fully manual (CAQH 2024).

Today a processor keys each form by hand, looks up the member, coverage and provider in separate systems, then accepts or bounces it. Around 8 minutes a claim, and errors become provider rework. With ClaimPilot, the system reads, checks and matches every claim and accepts the clear ones; processors handle only the exceptions, each with the reason attached. The 8 minutes and 5% are assumptions. Nationally only about 2% of medical claims are fully manual (CAQH 2024), so 5% is above average and the value slide shows the 2% case too. The pilot replaces both with a measured baseline.
